Full plans or building notice — which should I choose?

4 min read Updated June 2026

There are two main ways to apply for building regulations approval. Both are perfectly valid — the right one just depends on how much detail you want checked before work starts.

The short version

If you'd like your plans checked and approved before any work begins, choose full plans. If your project is small and straightforward and you're happy for it to be checked as the work happens, a building notice may be quicker to get going.

Full plans

You submit detailed drawings. We check them and issue approval before you build. Fewer surprises on site.

Building notice

You give notice and start sooner. Work is checked through site inspections as it progresses.

When full plans is the better choice

  • Larger or more complex projects, like a two-storey extension
  • When a lender, insurer or buyer may want to see formal approval
  • When you want certainty about costs before building begins

When a building notice can work

  • Small, familiar jobs such as removing a load-bearing wall
  • When an experienced builder is managing the work
  • When you'd rather not wait for plans to be checked first
Not sure? You can start an application and change the route later with your surveyor. Choosing one now doesn't lock you in.

What happens next

Once you've chosen, you'll add your project details and any documents, see a clear fee, and submit. We'll confirm we've received everything and tell you exactly what happens next — no jargon, no guessing.
